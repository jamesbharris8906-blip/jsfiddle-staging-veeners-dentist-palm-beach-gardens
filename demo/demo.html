A veneer is a thin shell, usually porcelain, bonded to the front of a tooth. It hides chips, cracks, stains that whitening cannot lift, small gaps, and teeth that sit slightly out of line. The dentist shapes each shell to match the color and shape of the teeth around it, so the result looks like natural teeth, only better. Treatment usually takes two visits: one to prepare the teeth and take a mold, and one to fit the finished shells. Temporary shells protect the teeth in between. With good care, porcelain shells can last ten years or more, and they resist stains far better than natural enamel.

General Dentistry

General dentistry keeps the rest of the mouth healthy, and it matters before any cosmetic work begins. Checkups, cleanings, digital X-rays, fillings, and gum care catch decay and gum problems that would ruin a new veneer if left alone. The team will not place veneers on a tooth with active decay or gum disease, and they explain why that order matters. Patients who keep up with routine care also keep their veneers longer, because clean gums and healthy enamel hold the shells better. A veneer is only as good as the tooth underneath it.

Emergency

Emergency visits cover severe tooth pain, broken or chipped teeth, knocked-out teeth, lost crowns or fillings, swelling, and bleeding that will not stop. A chipped front tooth on the morning of a big event is both an urgent problem and a cosmetic one, and the team can smooth a sharp edge the same day and plan the full repair once the tooth is safe. When you call, the team asks where it hurts, what happened, and whether you have swelling or a fever, then finds the earliest slot that day. Oral Surgery

Oral surgery covers extractions, wisdom teeth, and corrective procedures that routine care cannot solve. It rarely comes up in a veneer plan, but it matters when a damaged tooth cannot be saved or a wisdom tooth is crowding the front teeth. The team treats surgery with care: clear steps before the visit, steady hands during it, and written aftercare for home. Extractions are a last resort, and the dentist will only suggest one after explaining the other choices. After surgery, patients get guidance on pain relief, food, and what to watch for.

Dental Implants

Dental implants replace a missing tooth with a post in the jaw and a crown that looks and works like a real tooth. They matter for veneer patients too, because a missing tooth next to a veneered one throws off the whole smile. The office handles implant care from the first consult through placement and the final crown, and Dr. Weiss has years of hands-on work in this area. Implants keep nearby teeth from shifting, which protects the alignment of a new smile. If a tooth cannot be saved, an implant plus veneers on the teeth around it can rebuild the smile as a whole.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do veneers last?

Porcelain veneers usually last ten to fifteen years with good care. Brushing twice a day, flossing, and keeping up with cleanings protect both the shells and the teeth under them. Avoid biting hard objects like ice or pens, and wear a night guard if you grind your teeth. The dentist checks the shells at each visit and can repair or replace one that chips.

Will veneers change how I talk?

Most people adjust within a few days. The shells are thin, and the dentist shapes them to fit the way your lips and tongue move. A slight lisp can show up for a day or two while your mouth gets used to the new shape, and it fades on its own. Tell the dentist at the fitting if anything feels too thick or catches when you speak.

Do veneers need special care?

No special products are needed, but a few habits help. Brush twice a day with a soft brush, floss daily, and see the dentist every six months. Skip whitening toothpaste, which can scratch the porcelain, and do not use your teeth to open packages. If a shell feels loose or chips, call the office right away instead of waiting for your next visit.

Can veneers fix a gap between my front teeth?

Yes, for small gaps, veneers can close the space in two visits and give a straight-looking smile. Larger gaps or teeth that are far out of line are better fixed with Invisalign first, and the dentist will say so at the consult. Veneers hide the gap rather than moving the tooth, so the choice depends on how wide the gap is.

How much do veneers cost?

The price depends on how many teeth are treated and the material used. A single veneer costs less than a full set, and the office gives a written price at the consult before any work starts. Insurance rarely covers veneers because they are cosmetic, but financing through CareCredit and Cherry can spread the cost over months. Ask for the full picture, including the cost of the consult and any follow-up visits.

What if I grind my teeth at night?

Tell the dentist at the consult. Grinding can crack veneers, so the team will usually make a night guard to wear while you sleep. The guard protects the shells and the teeth under them. If the grinding is severe, the dentist may suggest treating it first, or choosing a stronger material for the shells.
